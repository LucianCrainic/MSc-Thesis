# Review of the parked literature chapter — 4 October 2026

## Decision

The existing chapter is useful source material, but its central argument
now points at a narrower thesis than the one being developed. It asks how
much faster an unmodified `llama.cpp` process runs in local HBM than in local
DDR, then treats weight/KV separation as a conditional fourth question.
The current experiment in `/Users/lucian/University/vllm` instead makes
placement and concurrent use of the two memory systems central. The new
review should be organized around **bandwidth, capacity, and access policy
for weights and KV state**, with the whole-process HBM/DDR contrast as a
calibration experiment.

**Working umbrella question:** On a Xeon Max CPU with HBM and DDR, how do
memory mode and placement/scheduling of model weights and KV blocks affect
inference latency and throughput across model size, context, and concurrency?
Subquestions must distinguish (1) Cache mode, where HBM is a transparent
memory-side cache, from (2) Flat mode, where HBM and DDR are separately
addressable and software can divide objects and work between them. These
are different treatments, not two ways to run the same placement policy.
[Intel's Xeon Max mode guide](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/vtune-profiler/cookbook/2023-2/profiling-hbm-performance-on-intel-max.html)
states that only DDR is visible in Cache mode and both tiers are visible
in Flat mode. The 7 September node check recorded that the CRESCO8 HBM nodes
were in Cache mode at that check. Recheck the mode on each later
allocation; Flat-mode research depends on an administrator-provided change.

## What the current draft gets right

- It separates prefill from decode and warns against predicting end-to-end
  speedup from STREAM ratios.
- It treats page residency, NUMA locality, capacity, traffic, and
  measurement uncertainty as necessary evidence.
- It cites [Fang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13231) for dynamic KV
  placement and [Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25353) for
  separating weight work from attention/KV work on CPUs. Both constrain
  a contribution claim.
- The close reading of Na et al. and Ibeid et al. remains valuable as
  platform context, provided their results are not treated as a direct
  prediction for this runtime and workload.

## Corrections before any prose returns to the thesis

1. **Change the organizing problem.** Most space now goes to making CPU
   inference credible and to arithmetic kernels. That is background,
   whereas the thesis question is how weights and KV traffic use two
   memory systems. Condense the kernel survey and expand sections on
   simultaneous tier access, attention partitioning, KV block policies,
   bandwidth balance, and capacity/bandwidth tradeoffs.
2. **Do not describe a Flat-mode experiment as presently executable on
   CRESCO8.** The September node check found Cache mode. In Cache mode,
   DDR is the visible main memory and HBM may still accelerate hits;
   calling it merely “DDR-like” would hide that effect. There is no
   separately bindable HBM NUMA target or same-node DDR-only control.
3. **Do not claim the tiered implementation is hypothetical.** The
   experimental vLLM branch already binds model loading to HBM, allocates
   fixed-tier KV blocks using `mbind`, fills HBM first, then uses smoothed
   tier timing to select new blocks near capacity. During dual-tier
   decode it can assign HBM and DDR attention work to separate OpenMP
   groups and merge partial outputs. It does not migrate active blocks.
   See `docs/design/cpu_tiered_kv.md` in that repository. Its current
   ablation reports little evidence yet for an adaptive scheduling gain:
   disabling the tier-aware attention schedule measured 303.5 ms mean
   TPOT in the documented comparison. Report this as an experiment to
   investigate, not a validated optimization.
4. **Correct the MedLocalGPT hardware.** The archived literature section
   calls its Xeon “Platinum 8592+”; the [paper](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4164/paper11.pdf)
   specifies **Platinum 8480+** on an AWS VM. Even with the corrected
   model, its results are contextual CPU `llama.cpp` observations, not a
   controlled DDR-only counterfactual for the CRESCO8 Max 9480.
5. **Narrow novelty wording.** [Fang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13231)
   explicitly frame dynamic KV placement as maximizing *aggregated*
   bandwidth under a capacity limit. The CPU substrate and measured
   Flat-mode implementation may differ, but the broad idea of using
   both tiers is established. Claims such as “the prediction exists but
   no implemented parallel tier attention does” also require checking
   the papers below.
6. **Avoid a single weight/KV preference for every workload.** The
   branch's weights-in-HBM choice has local measurements behind it, but
   longer contexts and greater concurrency can shift traffic toward KV.
   Compare all-HBM where feasible, all-DDR, weights-first, KV-first,
   fixed mixed splits, HBM-first feedback allocation, and scheduler
   enabled/disabled at equal cores, memory budget, model, and workload.

## Papers missing from the current chapter

| High | [Fang et al., dynamic KV placement (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13231) | Already cited but underused: aggregated bandwidth and capacity-constrained placement are the closest formal framing for the new question. Examine its objective and assumptions, not only its headline bound. |
| Medium | [Xiao et al., OasisKV (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08097) | vLLM-based KV placement beyond HBM using prediction and sparse prefetch. Its approximate attention and transfer hierarchy differ from this branch's full, direct CPU attention; compare the mechanisms and quality assumptions. |
| Medium | [Wei et al., HMA-Serve (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29986) | Phase-specific use of HBM- and GDDR-based accelerators. Relevant to the distinction between prefill and decode, but its inter-device transfer and economics should stay outside the direct CPU-tier comparison. |

These are candidates for full-text screening, not yet claims that their
experiments reproduce ours. Add bibliography entries only after checking
the complete methods, baselines, quality effects, and publication status.

## Proposed chapter structure after reading

1. **Memory demand of CPU LLM inference:** weight and KV bytes per token,
   phase, context, batch, precision, and the regime where each dominates.
2. **Xeon Max HBM/DDR mechanisms:** Flat and Cache modes, capacity,
   bandwidth, latency, NUMA locality, and concurrency needed to use both.
3. **Placement and scheduling systems:** static object placement,
   dynamic KV placement, parallel attention, offload/prefetch, and
   hardware-managed cache. Separate direct CPU access from GPU/PIM
   transfer hierarchies.
4. **Closest empirical evidence and limits:** Na, Ibeid, Reguly and
   relevant CPU systems; explain which numbers can and cannot be compared.
5. **Testable gap:** a controlled, phase-resolved assessment of mixed
   weight/KV residency and simultaneous HBM/DDR use on CPU, subject to
   Flat-mode access; a Cache-mode working-set study if Flat remains
   unavailable.
